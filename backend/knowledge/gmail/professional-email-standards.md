# Professional Email Writing Standards

## Guidelines
1. **Subject clarity**: Make the subject searchable, specific, and front-loaded with the key topic. Avoid all caps, excessive punctuation, spam-trigger phrasing, and overlong mobile-unfriendly subjects.
2. **Immediate purpose**: State the email purpose, ask, decision needed, or status in the first 1-2 body lines. Do not bury the reason for writing.
3. **Brevity and scannability**: Use short paragraphs, bullets or numbered lists for 3+ items, dates, options, or next steps, and minimal emphasis.
4. **Action-oriented language**: Clearly specify what action is needed, from whom, and by when. If no action is needed, say so explicitly.
5. **Tone control**: Match tone to the relationship and stakes. Prefer warm clarity over passive-aggressive politeness or excessive hedging.
6. **Accuracy checks**: Check names, company names, role titles, pronouns, dates, figures, attachment mentions, and recipient lists.
7. **Parseable delimiters**: Structure the final email with `[SUBJECT]`, `[GREETING]`, `[BODY]`, `[CLOSING]`, and `[SIGNATURE]`.

## Email Quality Checklist
- Ask or point is near the top.
- Subject is specific and searchable.
- One primary message or clearly numbered related items.
- Tone fits the recipient and situation.
- CTA or "No action needed" is explicit.
- Attachments and names are checked.
- The email would still read well if forwarded or screenshotted.
