# Email Writing Best Practices Guide

Use this guide only when the active platform is Gmail. ProPar should create a polished email, not a prompt. The final email must be practical, scannable, specific, and appropriate to the relationship and stakes.

## Universal Email Principles

### Subject Lines

- Be specific rather than vague. The subject should still make sense in search months later.
- Front-load the important word because mobile inboxes often truncate subject lines.
- Avoid all caps, excessive punctuation, and spam-trigger language such as "Free", "Act now", or "Guarantee".
- For internal or reply-thread emails, keep the subject stable unless the topic genuinely changed.
- For cold or first-touch emails, reference something specific to the recipient, their company, a recent post, or a mutual connection when available.

### Opening Lines

- State the reason for the email in the first sentence or two.
- Skip filler such as "I hope this email finds you well" unless the context is very formal or first-contact.
- Match formality to the relationship. A stranger may need a brief self-identification; a close colleague can get straight to the point.

### Structure And Length

- Use one primary ask or message per email. If there are multiple related topics, number them clearly.
- Use short paragraphs, usually 2 to 4 sentences.
- Put the ask, decision needed, deadline, or purpose near the top.
- Use bullets or numbered lists for 3 or more items, dates, options, requirements, or next steps.
- Keep the email as short as possible while still accomplishing the goal.

### Tone

- Match tone to relationship and stakes. Serious apologies need gravity; routine scheduling should not sound stiff or cold.
- Read for how the tone would land if the recipient were having a bad day. When interpersonal stakes exist, slightly favor warmth and clarity.
- Avoid excessive hedging such as "just", "sort of", "I think maybe", or "when you get a chance" unless uncertainty is real.
- Avoid passive-aggressive phrasing such as "Per my last email" or "As I mentioned previously". Restate the point plainly.

### Calls To Action

- Every email that needs a response should include a specific ask: what is needed, from whom, and by when.
- Prefer "Can you confirm by Thursday whether option A or B works for the launch date?" over "Let me know your thoughts."
- If no response is needed, state that clearly: "No action needed - just keeping you in the loop."

### Sign-Offs

- Match the sign-off to the situation: "Best", "Thanks", and "Regards" fit most professional emails.
- Use "Best regards" or "Warmly" for more formal or first-contact emails.
- Include relevant contact information for external recipients or when the recipient may need another way to reach the sender.

## Email Type Guidance

### Cold Outreach Or Sales

Goal: get a reply or meeting from someone who does not know the sender.

- Subject: specific, relevant, and not clickbait.
- Opening: one sentence establishing why this recipient, why this sender, and why now.
- Body: one clear value proposition tied to the recipient's likely situation. Avoid feature dumping.
- Length: target 75 to 125 words.
- CTA: low-friction and specific, such as "Worth a 15-minute call next week?"
- Avoid generic mail-merge language, overselling, and unsolicited large attachments.

### Follow-Up

Goal: resurface something without being annoying.

- Reference the original context in one line.
- Add something new, such as a data point, clearer ask, or deadline.
- Keep it shorter than the original email.
- After 2 to 3 unanswered follow-ups, close the loop politely instead of pinging indefinitely.

### Networking Or Introduction

Goal: build or activate a professional relationship.

- Lead with a specific, genuine reason for reaching out.
- Keep the ask small, such as a 15-minute call.
- For double opt-in introductions, ask permission first, then provide one or two sentences of context on each person.
- When responding to an intro, move the introducer to BCC and say so.

### Job Application Or Cover-Letter Email

Goal: get an interview.

- If a cover letter is attached, do not duplicate it in the email body.
- Use a subject like `Application: [Role] - [Your Name]` unless the posting specifies another format.
- State the role, one clear reason the candidate fits, and point to attachments.
- Proofread recruiter names, company names, and role titles carefully.
- Keep tone warm, professional, and not over-familiar.

### Customer Support Or Service

Goal: resolve an issue and preserve trust.

- Acknowledge first, explain second.
- Be concrete about next steps and timing.
- Avoid corporate hedge-speak. Prefer direct acknowledgment.
- If the issue cannot be fully solved, say what can be done and what happens next.
- For angry or escalated customers, avoid defensiveness and absolute claims.

### Internal Workplace Email

Goal: move work forward without unnecessary back-and-forth.

- Requests: state what is needed, why briefly, and by when in the first two lines.
- Status updates: lead with the headline, such as on track, at risk, or blocked.
- Delegation: specify scope, decision authority, and deadline.
- Use CC only for people who need visibility or action.

### Meeting Request Or Scheduling

Goal: get a meeting on the calendar with minimal back-and-forth.

- Propose specific times instead of asking "when are you free?"
- State purpose and expected duration in the first line.
- Include the expected outcome: decision, brainstorm, review, status update, or alignment.
- For recurring meetings, make cadence clear and provide an easy way to decline or suggest another cadence.

### Apology Or Difficult News

Goal: take responsibility and preserve the relationship.

- Lead with the apology or difficult news, not an excuse.
- Be specific about what went wrong and the impact.
- State what is being done to fix or prevent the issue.
- Avoid "sorry, but" because it weakens the apology.
- Keep the apology proportionate to the issue.

### Thank-You Or Appreciation

Goal: reinforce a relationship.

- Be specific about what the recipient did and why it mattered.
- Keep it short and genuine.
- Send it promptly, especially after interviews, favors, or gifts.

### Complaint Or Escalation

Goal: get a problem taken seriously and resolved.

- State the issue and impact factually before requesting a remedy.
- Include dates, order numbers, reference numbers, and prior correspondence.
- State exactly what resolution is requested.
- Use a factual paper-trail tone when further escalation is possible.

### Reminder Or Nudge

Goal: prompt action without nagging.

- Reference the original ask and deadline directly.
- Keep it brief.
- If time-sensitive, say so plainly.

### Announcement Or Broadcast

Goal: inform a broad audience clearly at a glance.

- Put the headline or decision in the first line.
- Use clear visual hierarchy with headings, short paragraphs, and sparing emphasis.
- If action is required, separate it from FYI information.
- For policy or process changes, state what is changing, why, when it takes effect, and where to ask questions.

### Resignation Or Sensitive HR

Goal: communicate professionally without burning bridges.

- Keep resignation emails short and factual: last day, transition support, and thanks.
- For sensitive HR topics, use email mainly to schedule a conversation when nuance matters.

### New Role Or Team Introduction

Goal: establish presence and make collaboration easy.

- Include brief background, what the sender will do, and how people can best reach or collaborate with them.
- A little personality is appropriate, but keep it professional.

## Subject Line Cheat Sheet

| Email type | Subject pattern |
|---|---|
| Cold outreach | `{Specific relevant hook} - {your name/company}` |
| Follow-up | `Re: {original subject}` or `Following up: {topic}` |
| Meeting request | `{Purpose} - {duration} - {proposed time}` |
| Status update | `{Project name}: {On track/At risk/Blocked}` |
| Request | `Need by {date}: {what you need}` |
| Apology | `Regarding {specific issue} - update` |
| Announcement | `{What's changing} - effective {date}` |
| Job application | `Application: {Role} - {Your Name}` |
| Thank-you | `Thank you - {specific thing}` |

## Common Mistakes To Avoid

1. Burying the ask.
2. Using the wrong name, company, pronoun, role, date, or merge field.
3. Passive-aggressive politeness.
4. Ambiguous deadlines such as "soon" or "when you get a chance".
5. Overlong subject lines that lose the key point on mobile.
6. Reply-all overuse.
7. Promising attachments but not attaching them.
8. Sending while upset without rereading.
9. Ending without a clear next step.
10. Formatting overload, such as too much bold, multiple fonts, or excessive color.

## ProPar Gmail Output Requirements

When ProPar creates `improvedPrompt` for Gmail:

- The field must be the finished email, not advice about the email.
- Always use these parseable delimiters in order:
  - `[SUBJECT] <subject line>`
  - `[GREETING] <greeting>`
  - `[BODY] <email body paragraphs>`
  - `[CLOSING] <closing phrase>`
  - `[SIGNATURE] <signature placeholder or sender name>`
- Choose the email type first, then apply the matching guidance.
- Put the point, ask, decision, or purpose in the first two lines of the body.
- Add an explicit CTA or explicit "No action needed" when appropriate.
- Use placeholders such as `[recipient name]`, `[date]`, `[attachment name]`, `[role title]`, or `[meeting times]` when needed.
- In `whatChanged`, mention concrete email upgrades such as "Clarified subject line", "Moved the ask near the top", "Added a specific deadline", "Matched tone to recipient and stakes", or "Removed passive-aggressive phrasing".
